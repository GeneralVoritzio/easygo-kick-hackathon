KICK Hackathon — Idea Scorer

Challenge 1: Hype Tracker / Stream Companion Overlay  ·  Sat 1 Aug 2026, Fortress Melbourne  ·  4–5 hrs build  ·  $50,000

Where the points actually are

50%
Decided before you arrive — Problem Solving (25) + Creativity (25). This is concept selection. It is the whole game.
30%
Nearly free — Presentation (10) + Collaboration (10) + UX (10). Independent of the idea. Pure preparation. Most teams drop these.
20%
Technical Execution. The only part that rewards ambitious code — and you have 4 hours. Do not spend the week here.
Read this twice: a modest idea built flawlessly caps out around 60. A sharp, original idea with a scrappy build and a rehearsed demo clears 80. Optimise for concept and delivery, not engineering scope.

Hard gates

Any unchecked box kills the idea outright. Don't negotiate with these — a blocked idea burns a week and dies on the day.

Weighted score

Two proxy questions per judging criterion. Score each 1–5. Be brutal — everything scores 4 when you're in love with it.

The formula

GATES = G1 · G2 · G3 · G4 · G5 · G6 (all must pass, else score = 0) RAW = Σ weight(c) × ( avg(q1,q2) / 5 ) for c in the 6 judging criteria weights: Product 25 · Creativity 25 · Technical 20 · Demo 10 · Team 10 · UX 10 FINAL = GATES × RAW × RISK RISK ∈ {1.00, 0.85, 0.65}

Ship anything above 75. Between 60–75, keep prototyping. Below 60, kill it Sunday night — no sunk-cost arguments.

The 20 ideas every other team will bring

Where your current three sit

  • Kick Director — collides with #12 and #17. A mod dashboard is the single most predictable answer to this challenge statement. Strong product thinking, weak originality, and dashboards demo badly (nothing moves on stage).
  • Extension library / plugin platform — genuinely original, but it's a platform, not a feature. It fails the 4-hour gate, and platforms demo as abstractions. The escape hatch: build one killer extension and imply the platform in the pitch.
  • Profile customisation / badges (wildcard) — collides with #11. Also not real-time, which is an explicit requirement ("reflects real-time stream behaviour").

None of these are dead — but none clear 75 as stated. That's the point of scoring them Sunday rather than defending them Friday.

Three angles the blocklist leaves open

  • Aim at the viewer who isn't watching. Every listed idea serves the streamer or the person already in chat. Nobody builds for the 90% who have the tab muted in the background.
  • Make the overlay the thing viewers compete over, not a readout the streamer consumes. Readouts are inert on stage; contested state is theatre.
  • Cross-platform is in your brief and nobody else's. You already flagged that streamers are simulcasting. A companion that survives off-Kick is a real differentiator and a real product argument.

Consolidation workflow

Rule zero: nobody discusses ideas in the group chat before Sunday's session. The first idea posted anchors everyone else, and you end up defending it instead of beating it. Diverge in private, converge in the room.

How the Sunday session runs (90 minutes)

  • 0–10 Everyone drops their 5 ideas into this tool cold. No pitching, no reactions.
  • 10–25 Gate pass only. Anything that fails a hard gate is out. Silent, fast, no defending.
  • 25–60 Score survivors together. One person drives, one person argues the low score for every question. If a score is unanimous first-try, you're not being honest.
  • 60–75 Rank. Take the top 3 into the week.
  • 75–90 Assign each top-3 an owner who has to bring a 45-minute clickable mock by Wednesday.

Rapid prototyping rule

45 minutes per mock, hard stop. If it isn't compelling as a rough mock, it will not be compelling as a polished build — polish adds maybe 10 points to a concept, never 30. Kill it and move on. You want to arrive on 1 Aug having killed at least four ideas.

The 30% almost nobody prepares

Presentation, Collaboration and UX are worth as much as Technical Execution and Creativity combined minus five points. They're checklist work. Tick every box and you bank 30 while other teams are still debugging.

The pre-building problem. Detailed specs, technical requirements and datasets are released on the day. Clause 9 warrants your submission is original work created by you and your teammates, and clause 5(a) requires good faith throughout. Turning up with a finished product is both a rules risk and a strategic one — if the released spec differs from your assumption, a pre-built solution is harder to pivot than an empty repo.

Safe to build this week

  • Repo boilerplate, build tooling, deploy target
  • Kick API auth flow working end-to-end (you don't want to burn hour one on OAuth)
  • A mock-data generator that fakes a live stream — chat velocity, events, viewer counts
  • A Kick-accurate design system: dark base, #53fc18, type scale, components
  • The pitch narrative, roles, and who speaks when

Build on the day

  • The feature itself. All of it. That's the part being judged and the part that has to match the released spec.

Other clauses worth knowing

  • 3(e) — Easygo allocates teams to challenges at its sole discretion, and each team does one challenge. Worth confirming in writing that you're locked to Challenge 1 before you spend the week on it.
  • 3(a)–(c) — Easygo forms the teams, and can amend them at any time. Build a plan that survives losing or gaining a person.
  • 3(h) — Final submission goes by email to events@easygo.io, and you must delete local copies afterwards. Have the packaging step ready before the clock runs out.
  • 3(g) — You must use the public API at docs.kick.com. Anything outside it needs to be materials Easygo expressly provided.
  • 7(d)–(e) — Kick gets a perpetual worldwide licence to your submission, and owns improvements to their materials outright. Fine for a hackathon; just don't bring an idea you were planning to commercialise.
  • Collaboration is 10% and explicitly looks for "evidence of a contribution from every team member." Commit history is that evidence. Everyone commits under their own name, and everyone speaks in the demo.
